## Issue 12: stage 1, rounded rectangles with verified gradients

Written 2026-11-27.

Machine: Apple M5 (Mac17,2), 10 cores, 16 GiB, macOS 27.0.1, Python 3.12.14, PyTorch 2.15.0, PySide6-Essentials 6.12.0.
Environment id `sha256:6b5d299af418a80202b1eafa91dbc1789f7df3cf640eddea7286f9138290ce58`, the id of the S2 record.

Every number below is from one run of `uv run core-ultra renderer check` on the committed configuration: `records/renderer/20261127T100441Z-stage-1-6b5d299af418.json`, tied to `records/doctor/20261127T100434Z-6b5d299af418.json`. The run takes about seven seconds.

### What runs

- **The training renderer**, `src/core_ultra/training_renderer.py`. `draw(elements, viewport, scale)` draws rounded rectangles with fill and opacity as a PyTorch image that gradients pass through. `elements_of(scene)` gives a validated scene's numbers as tensors in the scene's drawing order. `to_rgba8(image)` gives an image in the runtime renderer's form. It draws on the CPU and on the Apple GPU, one scene or a batch.
- **`core-ultra renderer check`**, which verifies it on the known scenes of `configs/renderer/check-v1.json` and writes one record holding the support matrix.

How a pixel is covered: the lesser of two areas, neither ever less than the true one. The first is the pixel's area inside the element's box, exact for any box without a corner radius. The second, at a corner, is the pixel's area on the near side of the straight line that touches the corner's arc nearest the pixel's centre. Elements are then composited one over the other in the order given.

### What was measured

- **Finite differences, CPU, 64-bit floats.** 880 numbers in 140 comparisons: five scenes, at 1 and 2 pixels per viewport unit, two scalars of the image, seven attributes. All pass. The largest error is 5.5e-6, where gradients run to the thousands; the worst error relative to its row's largest gradient is 2.0e-6.
- **Apple GPU path, 32-bit floats, forward alone.** 14 images. The largest difference from the CPU's image is 5.7e-6 of full scale; 1e-4 is allowed.
- **Apple GPU path, backward.** The same 880 numbers. The largest difference from the CPU's gradient is 0.0088, and the worst relative to its row's largest gradient is 8.7e-5. All pass.
- **Unsupported operations and CPU fallbacks: none.** The first run is made with the CPU fallback not allowed, whatever the caller's environment says, and it completed. A second run with the fallback allowed and PyTorch's profiler logging reported no fallback.
- **Moves.** 14 of 14: an element moved toward its target in eight steps (position, both axes at once, size, corner radius, fill, opacity, everything at once over a backdrop), at both scales. The render loss falls at every step, is exactly zero on the target, and its gradient points along the move at every step.
- **Zero gradients.** 8 of 8. Position has no gradient when a square-cornered element does not touch its target; nothing about an element under an opaque one has a gradient; nothing but opacity has one when the opacity is zero. In each the number is part of the computation, and the same loss with the number cut out is reported as disconnected, not as zero.

What the table says:

1. **Without a corner radius the two renderers agree to rounding**: 2.1 levels at most, dividers thinner than a pixel included. The mean of about 0.3 is the runtime renderer's rounding to 8 bits; the training renderer does not round.
2. **With a corner radius they differ by up to 34 levels on a few corner pixels**, and in no scene is more than 0.81% of the pixels over 16 levels apart.
3. **Most of that is the runtime renderer.** In `one-rounded` and `overlaps`, whose radii are 2.4 units and more, it is up to 31 levels from exact cover where the training renderer is within 10. This is reported and not fixed: see the first item under "What failed".
4. **The training renderer's own error is on small corners.** An arc drawn as its tangent covers too much where the arc turns sharply: up to 17 levels on corner pixels in `radii` and 13 in `thin`, the two scenes with radii under two pixels. Also reported and not fixed.

Borders, text and shadows are not drawn by this stage, so none of this compares them.

### What was tested

`uv run pytest`: 571 tests pass in 213 s (525 before this issue). 46 are new: 31 in `tests/test_training_renderer.py` and 15 in `tests/test_cli_renderer_check.py`.

The judges are not the renderer. Finite differences are taken by the test file and, in the check, by the check. Images are judged against the runtime renderer and against cover counted from sample points. The command-line tests run the real command and read the record it wrote; three of them provoke a failure (a configuration that states a gradient where there is none, a tolerance nothing can meet, a configuration that cannot be used) and assert on the exit status and on what is said.

### Decisions

- **The image is premultiplied RGBA.** A loss over pixels should not divide by alpha, which is zero wherever nothing is drawn. `to_rgba8` gives the runtime renderer's form for a person or a comparison.
- **Cover is areas, not a blurred edge.** A box's cover is exact, so a true scene's square-cornered elements are the runtime renderer's pixels. The cost is under "What failed": a gradient only where an outline crosses a pixel.
- **An arc is its tangent.** The first version took cover from the distance to the outline alone, which is exact only for an upright edge. The tangent's cover is exact for a straight edge at any angle. On one opaque rounded rectangle with a radius of 3 to 12 pixels, measured while building and not in the record, the largest difference from counted cover went from 9 to 14 levels down to 3 to 6.
- **An edge on a pixel boundary is counted once.** Autograd's own clamp would give a gradient from both pixels that meet at such an edge, twice the true one; true scenes are written on whole numbers, so this is the common case. `test_an_edge_on_a_whole_number_is_counted_once`.
- **`draw` validates nothing that carries a gradient.** A training step cannot stop to refuse a prediction. What each out-of-range value does is stated in `training_renderer.LIMITS`, is in the record, and is tested.
- **The finite-difference scalars have no kink of their own**: a squared difference and a weighted sum. The first version used the absolute difference, and failed on pixels that happened to equal their target, where the absolute value has no derivative. The render loss itself is checked by the moves.
- **Finite differences are not taken on kinks.** `whole-numbers` and `square-corners` are compared with the runtime renderer and, forward only, on the Apple GPU path. A gradient on a kink is tested by `test_an_edge_on_a_whole_number_is_counted_once`, against the area it must give.
- **A check scene's numbers are kept off the places where the cover changes formula.** One scene's corner centre fell exactly on a pixel centre at scale 2, and its central difference was wrong in proportion to the step while the gradient was right. The radius was changed from 0.6 to 0.63, and the configuration says why.
- **The check's known scenes are its own**, small and off the pixel grid, and not dataset records: a dataset record has borders, text and shadows, which this stage does not draw.
- **The Apple GPU path is run in a process of its own**, twice: once with the CPU fallback not allowed, once with it allowed and PyTorch's profiler logging. What PyTorch prints about the Apple GPU path is now read in one place, `apple_gpu_path.py`, which `measure step` uses too; its record is unchanged.
- **Exact cover is counted from points no two of which share a column or a row of the pixel.** A square grid of 16 by 16 points counts an upright edge to one part in 16 only: against it the runtime renderer seemed 8.1 levels off on `thin` at scale 2, and against these points it is 4.2. These are right to one part in 256 on an upright edge.

### What failed, and what limits this

- **The runtime renderer's rounded corners are up to 31 levels from exact cover.** Found here, by measuring both renderers against cover counted from sample points. It is on a few pixels of each corner, and the size of the error changes with the radius and the scale in no order, which is what a curve drawn as a few straight pieces would give. The cause is not established. It is reported as [issue #37](https://github.com/robdhyman/core-ultra-neural-engine/issues/37) and not fixed here: a fix changes every reference image and every committed image hash. It does not reach training, because the render loss compares the training renderer with itself.
- **A corner of radius under about two pixels is drawn too full**, by up to 17 levels on its own pixels. A square corner is not affected; a small corner drawn at a low scale is. Which visual presets that touches at the scale training draws at is not measured.
- **A rounded element's whole cover changes a little with where its corners fall among the pixels.** So its position has a small gradient even where it touches nothing: 4.6e-5 against 2.9e-3 for its width in the record's case `rounded-without-overlap`, about 1.6%.
- **A gradient in position, size or corner radius comes only from pixels the outline crosses.** An element that shares no differing pixel with its target is not pulled toward it. The structure loss is what places an element; the render loss can only refine it. Issue #18 should expect this.
- **A corner radius past half the shorter side has no gradient and cannot come back.** Gradient descent stepped a radius past its bound in a test here and it stayed. The scene model's output for it has to be kept inside the bound. `test_a_corner_radius_past_its_bound_has_no_gradient_and_its_box_takes_it`.
- **The render loss can lower itself by making an element vanish.** With fill and opacity free, gradient descent took an element that half overlapped its target to an opacity of zero. That is the loss and not the renderer, and it is for issue #18.
- **Every element is drawn over the whole image.** No element is limited to the pixels near it, so a draw costs elements times pixels. Not measured at a dataset's size: the largest image here is 144 by 96 pixels with seven elements.
- **A batch shares one viewport size.** Scenes of different sizes are drawn one at a time.
- **Not measured**: time and memory of a draw; any scene with more than eight elements; scales below 1.
